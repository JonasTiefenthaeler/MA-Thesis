\chapter{Introduction}\label{chap: Introduction}

Turbulent flows are present in almost all engineering applications.
Especially turbulent flows over a wall, so called wall-bounded turbulent flows, are encountered frequently in all kinds of technical applications ZITAT.
Wall-bounded turbulence is of great interest in research and technology, since around $50 \%$ of the energy usage in flows through pipes or the movement of cars or planes through air can be accounted to the energy dissipation of wall-bounded turbulence \citep{jimenezCascadesWallBoundedTurbulence2012}.
In order to reduce this number, a improved understanding of fluid dynamics in general and especially turbulence behaviour at the wall and in the near wall region is of exceptional importance.

To increase the understanding of fluid dynamics, among others, the use of \acrfull{cfd} is a vital tool in fluid dynamics research.
Especially \acrfull{dns} and \acrfull{les} are a common choice among researchers, since they can yield important insight into the behaviour of turbulent flow \citep{moinDIRECTNUMERICALSIMULATION1998}.
However, also the simulation \acrfull{rans} is a common choice, if the application and optimization of engineering tasks is the goal.

In many real-world applications, wall roughness has a major impact on the drag, e.g.\ in shipping and aviation.
In shipping, the biofouling induced roughness can increase the overal drag by $\approx 10 \%$ \citep{flackReviewHydraulicRoughness2010} 
In aviation, even the comparatively low roughness still accounts for $\approx 5.5 \%$ of drag \citep{chungPredictingDragRough2021}. 

In order to improve the understanding of turbulence behaviour in rough walled conditions, many studies have been conducted.
A notable early experimental study was carried out by~\citeauthor{StroemungsgesetzeNikuradse}, who investigated the behaviour of turbulent flows in rough pipes.
The roughness was created by sand grains, that were attached to the pipe wall. 
Using the data of~\citeauthor{StroemungsgesetzeNikuradse}, the concept of the equivalent sand grain roughness was developed by~\cite{schlichtingExperimentelleUntersuchungenRauhigkeitsproblem1936}.
More recent experimental efforts were conducted by~\cite{schultzRoughwallTurbulentBoundary2007a} and~\cite{frohnapfelFlowResistanceHeterogeneous2024} who used sand paper as the roughness geometry.
Also, more recently experimental studies for generated irregular rough surface were conducted, in which the surfaces were generated using an Algorithm and produced by a 3D-printer \citep{flackSkinFrictionMeasurements2020}.

There were also substantial efforts to investigate the behviour of turbulence over rough surfaces using simulations.
Since the behaviour of the turbulence is of interest, all studies are conducted using the \acrshort{dns} method.
Turbulent flows over rough surfaces are mostly studied in the canonical channel case, e.g.\ by \citep{thakkarInvestigationTurbulentFlow2017,flackSkinFrictionMeasurements2020,kimTurbulenceStatisticsFully1987,jellyReynoldsDispersiveShear2018}, while the turbulent rough pipe has received less attention \citep{chanSystematicInvestigationRoughness2015,demaioDirectNumericalSimulation2023}.
Some studies used isolated roughness elements to find the impact of individual roughness features \citep{chatzikyriakouDNSTurbulentFlow2015}, while others tried to investigate the real world turbulence behaviour by using scanned surfaces of real objects \citep{thakkarSurfaceCorrelationsHydrodynamic2017,flackSkinFrictionMeasurements2020,frohnapfelFlowResistanceHeterogeneous2024}.
Furthermore, both regular and irregular roughness has been investigated.
Regular roughness e.g.\ by~\cite{leonardiStructureTurbulentChannel2004} who placed square bars onto a flat surface, and~\citeauthor{chanSystematicInvestigationRoughness2015} who investigated turbulent pipe flow with a sinusoidal surface \citep{chanSystematicInvestigationRoughness2015,chanSecondaryMotionTurbulent2018}.
Irregular roughness e.g.\ by~\cite{thakkarInvestigationTurbulentFlow2017,busseReynoldsnumberDependenceNearwall2017} and~\cite{demaioDirectNumericalSimulation2023}, who all used generated surfaces.
However, no general law exists yet to predict flow characteristics, e.g.\ drag or roughness function, directly from the surface topography \citep{chungPredictingDragRough2021}.
