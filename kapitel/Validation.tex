\chapter{Validation} \label{chap: Validation}
To test the simulation methods and chosen tools, validation simulations are performed.
The obtained results are compared to data from common literature, to ensure validity.
Furthermore, a convergence study is conducted, to test the grid resolution, as well as the time convergence of the statistics.
 
\section{Smooth wall pipe validation}\label{sec: Smooth wall pipe}
To validate the simulation setup, simulations of smooth wall turbulent pipes are performed.
The simulations in the smooth pipe are performed using the Reynolds-numbers $Reb \ 5300$ and $19000$.
Both of the used Reynolds-numbers here are also employed in the rough-wall simulations.
The simulation setup is described in \autoref{sec: Simulation setup}.

The results of the validation case using a bulk Reynolds-number $Re_b = 5300$ can be seen in \autoref{fig: results smooth 5700Reb}.
All data is compared to the equivalent case of~\cite{yaoDirectNumericalSimulations2023}.
The mesh refinement is chosen to be quite coarse, since it is the same mesh used for the NEKO turbulent pipe example.
A low computational effort was very important for the example case, hence the coarse grid spacings.
This is also the reason for the small deviations between the present data and the reference data.
The velocity profiles in \autoref{subfig: 5700 validation velocity profile} show very similar behavior between the present data and the reference data.
Also, the Reynolds stresses in \autoref{subfig: 5700 validation Reynolds stress} show similar trends, especially for the $v'v'$, $w'w'$ and $u'v'$ quantities.
However, the $u'u'$ stresses are underpredicted at their peak at $\approx y^+ 15$.
As mentioned before, this is expected due to the coarse mesh.
The viscous stress in \autoref{subfig: 5700 validation total stress} shows very similar behavior, while the total stress is affected by the already described deviations of the Reynolds shear stresses.
The streamwise $uu$ energy budgets in \autoref{subfig: 5700 validation budgets} show reasonably good agreement overall.
Only the dissipation term and viscous diffusion terms are underestimated slightly in the near wall region.
However, the energy residual shows the correct behavior of being zero at any wall normal position.

\section{Convergence studies} \label{sec: convergence studies}
A grid resolution as well as time convergence study is conducted to find reasonable parameters for both.
They sould yield sufficient results while keeping the necessary computational resources manageable.
The study was conducted using $Re_b = 5700$ in order to reduce the computational effort.
For all cases of the convergence study, the simulation setups for the rough pipe, explained in \autoref{sec: Simulation setup}, is used.

\subsection{Grid refinement study}\label{subsec: grid refinement study}
For the grid refinement study, three meshes of varying resolutions are used.
The resolution are altered in streamwise and azimuthal direction, while keeping the wall-normal resolution fixed.
Also, the polynomial order is kept constant at $p = 7$.
The used parameters are listed in \autoref{tab: parameters grid refinement study}.

The statistics were taken for $250$ time units, hence between $500 - 750$ time units.

The mean streamwise velocity profiles in \autoref{subfig: grid convergence uplus} show nearly no influence of the grid resolution, which indicates little affect of grid resolution on first order statistics, within reasonable bounds ZITAT.
The Reynolds stresses in \autoref{subfig: grid convergence RS} show partially a small dependence on grid resolution.
The values for $R_{vv}, R_{ww}$ and $R_{uv}$ are almost unaffected.
However, $R_{uu}$ shows a steady increase in its maximum value between $4.06$ and $4.17$ for increasing finer meshes.
This results in a difference of $~ 2.6\%$ between the highest and lowest value.
The total stress in \autoref{subfig: grid convergence total stress} seems to be nearly unaffected by the grid resolution. 
All meshes, including the coarsest mesh shows the expected slope of $1$ in the center region of the pipe.
Additionally, the trends for the viscous stress are identical.
However, there are slightly different trends for the Reynolds stress and the dispersive stress.
The dispersive stress decreases with increasing mesh resolution.
The finest mesh shows the lowest dispersive stress, especially in the transition and log-law region. 
The curves collapse onto another again near the center, when trending towards zero.
Also in the near-wall region, they behave very similar.\\
From the grid convergence study can be concluded, that the influence on the shown statistics is small for the used meshes.
However, it is expected, that the differences will grow with higher Reynolds numbers ZITAT.
For that reason, the coarsest mesh is not chosen for the study. 
The number of elements is $~64\%$ higher for the finest mesh compared to the intermediate one and thus a substantial increase in computational time is expected.
Since a sufficient convergence is shown for the intermediate resolution it is the chosen mesh for the following studies.

\subsection{Time convergence study}\label{subsec: Time convergence study}
Using the chosen mesh from \autoref{subsec: grid refinement study}, the time convergence is tested.
This is done to find the necessary initial transition time to statistically homogeneous turbulence, at which the computation of averaged values can start.
Also, the necessary duration of the averaging procedure is to be tested.
The time interval at which statistics are computed is from $300-750$ time units.
Every $100$ time units, averaged files were written, such that the flow behavior can be studied in time intervals of $100$ time units.
The studied averaging times are $300-500, 300 - 600, 300-750$ and $500-750$.

It becomes clear, that the influence of the averaging times on the statistics is very little.
Only minor differences are existent and if they are, they are minor.
This indicates validity of the chosen averaging time in \autoref{subsec: grid refinement study}.
Furthermore, it can be seen, that averaging until $750$ time units is not necessary to obtain valid statistics.
Eventhough the results shown here support the assumption, that an averaging time from $300-500$ time units is sufficient, it is not chosen for the further study.
The following study is conducted using averaging between $300-600$ time units throughout, since the time convergence study is performed using $Re_b = 5700$ and higher bulk Reynolds number will be used in the future.
To that extent to increase the confidence intervals, a longer averaging time is chosen.
