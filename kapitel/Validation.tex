\chapter{Validation} \label{chap: Validation}
To test the simulation methods and the selected tools, validations of the simulations are performed.
The obtained results are compared to data from the relevant literature to ensure validity.
Furthermore, a convergence study is conducted to test the grid resolution, as well as the time convergence of the statistics.
 
\section{Smooth wall pipe validation}\label{sec: Smooth wall pipe}
To validate the simulation setup, simulations of smooth wall turbulent pipes are performed.
The simulations in the smooth pipe are performed using the Reynolds numbers $Reb \ 5300$ and $19000$.
Both of these Reynolds numbers will be also employed in the rough-wall simulations.
The simulation setup is described in \autoref{sec: Simulation setup}.

The results of the validation case employing a bulk Reynolds number $Re_b = 5300$ can be seen in \autoref{fig: 5300 validation}.
A comparison of all data is made with the equivalent case of~\cite{yaoDirectNumericalSimulations2023}.
The mesh refinement is chosen to be quite coarse, since it is the same mesh used for the Neko turbulent pipe example.
In the context of the example case, it was of high priority to minimise computational effort, thus using coarse grid spacings.
This is also the reason for the small discrepancies observed between the present data and the reference data.
The velocity profiles in \autoref{subfig: 5300validation velocity profile} displays a high degree of similarity between the present data and the reference data.
In addition, the Reynolds stresses in \autoref{subfig: 5300validation Reynolds stress} show similar trends, particularly for the $v'v'$, $w'w'$ and $u'v'$ quantities.
However, the $u'u'$ stresses are shown to be underpredicted at their peak at $\approx y^+ 15$.
As mentioned before, this behaviour is expected due to the coarse mesh.
The viscous stress in \autoref{subfig: 5300validation total stress} shows very similar behaviour, while the total stress is affected by the previously described deviations of the Reynolds shear stresses.
The streamwise $uu$ energy budgets are presented in \autoref{subfig: 5300validation budgets}.
They show reasonable overall agreement.
Only the dissipation term and viscous diffusion terms are underestimated slightly in the near-wall region.
However, the energy residual displays the correct behaviour of being zero at any wall normal position.

\section{Convergence studies} \label{sec: convergence studies}
A grid resolution study and time convergence study are conducted to find reasonable parameters for both.
It is imperative that the results are sufficiently accurate while maintaining the required computational resources within acceptable limits.
The study was conducted using $Re_b = 5300$ in order to reduce the computational effort.
For all cases of the convergence study, the simulation setup for the rough pipe, explained in \autoref{sec: Simulation setup}, is used.

\subsection{Grid refinement study}\label{subsec: grid refinement study}
For the grid refinement study, three meshes of varying resolutions are used.
The resolutions are altered in both the streamwise and azimuthal directions, while maintaining the wall-normal resolution constant.
Also, the polynomial order is kept constant at $p = 7$.

As demonstrated in \autoref{subfig: grid convergence uplus}, the mean streamwise velocity profiles show minimal sensitivity to the grid resolution.
This indicates only minor affects of grid resolution on first order statistics, provided the parameters remain within reasonable bounds.
This conclusion is consitent with the findings in~\citep{moinDIRECTNUMERICALSIMULATION1998}.
The Reynolds stresses in \autoref{subfig: grid convergence RS} show, partially, a small dependence on grid resolution.
The values for $R_{vv}, R_{ww}$ and $R_{uv}$ are almost unaffected.
However, $R_{uu}$ shows a steady increase in its maximum value between $4.06$ and $4.17$ for increasing finer meshes.
This results in a difference of $~ 2.6\%$ between the highest and lowest value.
The total stress in \autoref{subfig: grid convergence total stress} appears to be nearly unaffected by the grid resolution. 
All meshes, including the coarsest mesh, show the expected slope of $1$ in the centre region of the pipe.
Additionally, the trends for the viscous stress are identical.
However, we observe slightly different trends for the Reynolds stress and the dispersive stress.
The dispersive stress decreases with increasing mesh resolution.
The finest mesh demonstrates the lowest dispersive stress, especially in the transition and log-law region. 
The curves converge into one another at the centre when trending towards zero.
Furthermore, in the near-wall region, they behave very similar.
As shown in \autoref{subfig: mesh convergence velocity defect}, the velocity defect exhibits only marginal dependence of the velocity on the grid resolution in the region $ 0.2 < \frac{y}{R} < 0.8$. 
The findings of the grid convergence study indicate that the influence on the demonstrated statistics is negligible for the used meshes.
However, it is expected that the discrepancies will increase with higher Reynolds numbers.
For that reason, the coarsest mesh is excluded from the study. 
The number of elements is $~64\%$ higher for the finest mesh compared to the intermediate one.
Thus, a substantial increase in computational time is expected.
The simulation of the coarsest mesh required approximately $6390$ core hours, the intermediate mesh approximately $8200$ and the fines mesh required approximately $14040$ core hours.
Due to the substantial increase in computational cost between the intermediate and finest mesh of approximately $71\%$, and a sufficient convergence is shown for the intermediate mesh resolution, it is the selected mesh for our following studies.

\subsection{Time convergence study}\label{subsec: Time convergence study}
The time convergence is tested using the mesh selected from \autoref{subsec: grid refinement study}.
This is done to determine the initial transition time to statistically homogeneous turbulence, at which the computation of averaged values can start.
In addition, the necessary duration of the averaging procedure is to be tested.
The time interval over which statistics are computed extends from $300$ to $750$ time units.
At intervals of $100$ time units, averaged files were written, thus enabling the flow behaviour to be studied in time intervals of $100$ time units.
The averaging times at which the study was conducted ranged from $300-500, 300 - 600, 300-750$ and $500-750$, respectively.

It is evident that the impact of the averaging times on the statistics is negligible.
Only minor discrepancies exist, and if such discrepancies are present, they are insignificant.
This indicates the validity of the chosen averaging time in \autoref{subsec: grid refinement study}.
Furthermore, it can be observed, that averaging until $750$ time units is required to obtain valid statistics.
Despite the findings presented here support the assumption that an averaging time from $300-500$ time units is adequiate, this time frame will not be utilised in the further study.
The following study is conducted using an averaging between $300$ and $600$ time units throughout, since the time convergence study is performed using $Re_b = 5300$ and higher bulk Reynolds number will be used in future research.
To that extent, to increase the confidence intervals, a longer averaging time is selected.
