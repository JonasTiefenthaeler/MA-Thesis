\chapter{Conclusion and outlook}\label{chap: Conclusion}

In this chapter this thesis is summarised.
Following, a conclusion of the findings is drawn.
Finally, an outlook is provided and suggestions for future work is given.

In this thesis turbulent pipes with rough surfaces are studied using \acrshort{dns}.
The theoretical background to wall-bounded turbulent flows is presented, followed by a brief introduction into the behaviour of turbulent flows over rough surfaces.
For the roughness, artificial surfaces are used, which are randomly generated using an algorithm, witch takes a random distribution and a power spectrum as the input parameters.
The rough surface is applied onto the pipe mesh using a Fourier decomposition and reconstruction of the generated rough surface.
The simulation setup is validated using smooth pipe \acrshort{dns} simulations at $Re_\tau = 180$ and $Re_\tau = 550$.
Additionally, both a mesh- and time convergence study is conducted.
Three different surfaces are examined.
Surface $S1$ exhibits the largest roughness height.
Surface $S2$ is of reduced roughness height, but keeps the effective slope of $S1$.
This made it necessary to use a higher number of Fourier modes to recreate the surface, which leads to a shorter wavelength of the rough surface.
Surface $S3$ exhibits the same shape as $S1$, but a uniform scaling factor is applied, such that the same roughness height as for $S2$ is attained.
The \acrshort{dns} studies are conducted using three different Reynolds numbers, namely $Re_b = 5300$, $Re_b = 11700$ and $Re_b = 19000$.
Those are chosen, since they are typical Reynolds numbers in the canonical smooth pipe case.
The behaviour of the surfaces at a fixed Reynolds numbers is compared.
Additionally, the Reynolds number influence on the turbulent flow over the three surfaces is studied.
The results are presented using first- and second-order statistics.
Furthermore, the energy spectra for all nine cases are presented.
A correlation between the viscous drag, roughness height and effective slope is drawn.

This thesis provides additional data, to further deepen the understanding the turbulence behaviour over rough surfaces.
All nine conducted simulations are in the transitionally rough regime.
Within this regime, a strong influence of the roughness height in first-and second order statistics, as well as the energy budgets is determined.
This is evident in the growth of the roughness functions $\Delta U^+$ with rising $k^+$ being faster for higher roughness height surfaces.
The effective slope has only a small influence on this function. 
Furthermore, the provided energy spectra demonstrated an increasing separation of scales for rising Reynolds number.
This indicates the existence of large scale motions for high Reynolds number turbulent flows over rough surfaces.
Also, a connection between the roughness height, effective slope and viscous drag is drawn.
A clear correlation emerges between the roughness height and viscous drag for positively sloped surfaces.

Based on this thesis, various possibilities for future research are enabled.
Given sufficient computational resources, a wide range parameter study to investigate the influence of individual roughness parameters is enabled using the provided combination of the used surface generation algorithm and mesh mapping method.
Furthermore, given the relatively large roughness cases in this work, a more thorough study of the secondary motions could yield additional insight intro the behaviour of turbulent flow within the roughness canopy.
Additionally, an investigation on the relation between the curvature of the roughness features and viscous- and pressure drag properties could yield additional insight about the flow separation of turbulent flows in rough conditions.

All in all, this thesis achieved its goal of providing insight to the turbulence behaviour in rough pipes in the transitionally rough regime.
