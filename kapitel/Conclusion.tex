\chapter{Conclusion and outlook}\label{chap: Conclusion}

In this chapter this thesis is summarised.
Following, a conclusion of the findings is drawn.
Finally, an outlook is provided and suggestions for future work is given.

In this thesis turbulent rough pipes with rough surfaces are studied using \acrshort{dns}.
Compared to many studies in literature, a body fitted mesh is used.
Artificial surfaces are used, which are randomly generated using a random distribution and a power spectrum.
The rough surface is applied onto the pipe mesh using a Fourier decomposition of the generated rough surface and mapping the Fourier modes onto the pipe mesh.
Three different surfaces are examined.
Surface $S1$ exhibits relatively large roughness height.
The other two surfaces are of reduced roughness height.
Surface $S2$ shows similar surface parameters then $S1$ but with reduced roughness height.
This made it necessary to use a higher number of Fourier modes to recreate the surface, which leads to a shorter wavelenght of the roughness height.
Surface $S3$ exhibits the same shape as $S1$, but a uniform scaling factor is applied, such that the same roughness height as for $S2$ is reached.
The sudies are conducted using three different Reynolds numbers, namely $Re_b = 5300$, $Re_b = 11700$ and $Re_b = 19000$.
Those are chosen, since they are typical Reynolds numbers in the canonical smooth pipe case.
The results are presented using first- and second-order statistics.
Furthermore, the energy spectra for all nine cases are presented.
A correlation between the viscous drag, roughness height and effective slope is drawn.
A comparison between the surfaces is conducted.
Additionally, the behaviour of the surfaces at varying Reynolds numbers is examined.

Conclusion

Bases on this thesis, various possibilities for future research are enabled.
The combination of the used algorithm for the surface generation and the provided application onto the mesh, a simple workflow for the generation of multiple irregular rough surfaces with almost arbitrarily chosen surface parameters is given.
If sufficient computational resources are at hand, an extensive study on the influence of the surface parameters is possible.
Furthermore, given the large roughness cases in this work, a more thorough study of the secondary motions could yield additional insight intro the behaviour of turbulent flow over large roughness.
